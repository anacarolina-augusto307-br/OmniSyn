## OmniSyn
OmniSyn is an open-source Python platform for microbial genomics, metagenomics, and synthetic biology exploration. Designed for education, exploratory research, and bioinformatics prototyping, it integrates sequence analysis, quality-control workflows, and interactive visualizations into a single, accessible environment.

The platform includes tools for ORF prediction, GC-content profiling, codon usage analysis, sequence alignment, FASTQ quality assessment, and genomic data visualization. Through its Streamlit-based web interface, OmniSyn aims to make computational microbiology more approachable while encouraging reproducible and transparent analytical practices.

## Author
OmniSyn was developed by a first-year undergraduate student with a strong interest in microbiology, computational biology, and bioinformatics. The project was created as a hands-on initiative to explore microbial genomics through software development and data analysis.

## Inspiration
The idea behind OmniSyn emerged from an interest in forensic investigations and the role of biological evidence in solving complex cases. Exploring how DNA and genomic data can reveal hidden information inspired the development of a platform focused on sequence analysis, microbial genomics, and computational biology.

## Project Vision
OmniSyn was created with the goal of making computational microbiology more accessible. By combining bioinformatics tools, interactive visualizations, and educational workflows, the platform aims to bridge the gap between biological sciences and programming.

## Why "OmniSyn"?
The name OmniSyn combines:
- Omni (Latin for "all" or "universal")
- Syn (inspired by synthesis, synthetic biology, and biological systems)

Together, the name reflects the project's goal of integrating multiple computational approaches into a unified platform for biological sequence analysis and exploration.

## Personal Motivation
OmniSyn began as a personal learning project driven by curiosity about how biological information can be decoded through computation. Inspired by forensic science, microbial genomics, and synthetic biology, the project serves as both a technical challenge and a platform for continuous scientific exploration.
